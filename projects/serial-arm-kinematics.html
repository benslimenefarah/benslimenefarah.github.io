01Forward kinematics with dual quaternions

For the 7-DOF RAFA-X1, each revolute joint is a unit dual quaternion built from its axis u and a point q on it, and the forward kinematics is their product of exponentials:

X(θ) = X₁(θ₁) X₂(θ₂) … X₇(θ₇) X₀, with Xᵢ = (cos θᵢ/2, sin θᵢ/2 · u) + ε (0, sin θᵢ/2 · (q × u))
  • Rotation and translation travel together in 8 numbers, and composing poses is a single product, with no 4×4 matrices.
  • Each point of the arm is moved by the joints before it, and its position is read back as t = 2 q_D q_R*.
  • Checked in Octave against the 4×4 matrix exponentials: the joint positions agree to 10⁻¹¹ mm on random poses.

02Forward velocity kinematics

Jacobians and velocity kinematics for the Franka and RAFA-X1 arms: how joint speeds map to the end-effector's linear and angular velocity.

03Numerical inverse kinematics

  • Franka (7 DOF): the pose error is turned into a twist with the matrix logarithm, ξ = vee(log(H⁻¹ H_d)), then the joints are updated with a damped pseudo-inverse of the Jacobian until ‖ξ‖ < ε.
  • UR10 and KUKA LWR4: IK posed as non-linear least squares on a 6-D position + orientation error and solved with fsolve; reusing the previous solution as the initial guess keeps motion continuous along a trajectory.
  • UR3e (lab): damped least squares on the spatial Jacobian with the product-of-exponentials model, Δθ = α Jᵀ(JJᵀ + λ²I)⁻¹ e.

04Heuristic IK: FABRIK

Forward And Backward Reaching IK on a 3D chain of six unit links. Instead of Jacobians, each pass is geometric: a forward pass pulls the tip onto the target, a backward pass puts the base back at the origin, and the two are repeated until the tip is close enough. I used it to reach a fixed target and to track one moving on a circle (the video above).

05Joint-space control

Proportional setpoint regulation, θ̇ = K_p (θ_d − θ), giving an exponentially decreasing error. Implemented and animated for a planar 2-DOF arm and for the Adept Viper.

06Cartesian pose control on SE(3)

The Adept Viper s650 (6 DOF, DH model) is driven to a full target pose H_d, position and orientation, instead of target joint angles. The pose error is a rigid transform; the matrix logarithm turns it into a twist that the Jacobian can map back to the joints.

pose errorH_e = H_d H⁻¹ in SE(3)
→
log mapξ = log(H_e)^∨ = (ω, v)
→
damped inverseθ̇ = λ Jᵀ(JJᵀ + μI)⁻¹ ξ
→
integrateθ ← θ + θ̇ Δt until ‖ξ‖ < ε
  • Closed-form logarithm: the angle comes from cos φ = (tr R − 1)/2, ω from (R − Rᵀ), and v = A⁻¹(φ) p. The cases φ ≈ 0 and φ ≈ π are handled separately so the twist stays defined.
  • Spatial Jacobian: each column is [z_{i−1}; z_{i−1} × (p_e − p_{i−1})], ordered (ω, v) to match the twist.
  • Robustness: damping μ = 0.01 bounds the joint speeds near singularities, and the speeds are clamped to ±2 rad/s like real actuator limits.
Run in Octave 10.3: from a target up to 20° away on each joint, it converges in 1708 steps (8.5 s at Δt = 5 ms, λ = 1, ε = 10⁻³) and stops 1.0 mm and 0.003° from the target pose.
